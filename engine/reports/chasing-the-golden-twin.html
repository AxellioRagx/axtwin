Chasing the Golden Twin

Nineteen iterations spent casting a synthetic replica of a live corporate network from its own packet capture, deploying it to a real cluster, capturing it back through the same tap, and measuring the gap. The twin got measurably closer, then stopped. The reason it stopped turned out to be more useful than any score it reached, and it changed what “golden” means.

CorpAPCN → corpapcn-twin 138 VMs · 27 segments · 24 personas · 15-host internet sink 2026-08-27 → 09-03

Verdict · after iteration 19

The loop is closed, and not because the twin is golden. The axis that decides convergence oscillated in a band for six straight iterations. Iteration 19 hit its own target and regressed two of its three must-not-regress axes, which the run's own rule calls a trade. The mechanism is measurable: two thirds of the source's local edge kinds cannot exist in the twin at all, so every tuning pass re-weights the third that can.

The most useful output is the correction to the objective. A traffic-shape distance is neither the goal, an attack-and-defense range, nor a proxy for what that goal depends on. Fidelity is now a closed specification, and the work has moved to the half of it the wire cannot see.

↓ TVD = total variation distance. It is a distance, not a score: 0.000 is identical, 1.000 is disjoint. Lower is better on every number below — read 0.016 as “about 1.6% of the twin's hosts would have to change role to match”. Its companion, Jaccard, runs the other way — see the two directions.

01What the loop actually does

A digital twin is only worth having if it behaves like the thing it copies. This loop exists to put a number on “behaves like”.

The source is CorpAPCN, a live corporate network observed through a packetXpress capture appliance. aximprint ingests that capture with Zeek, infers an entity/role/segment model, and casts a twin: a twin-config, a set of synthesised traffic personas, and a tap plan. axRange deploys the twin onto a Proxmox cluster, mirrors every host port back to the same appliance, and the capture is pulled back out and run through the identical pipeline.

That last part is the whole design. Both sides go through the same Zeek → ingest → materialise path, so any difference in the resulting fingerprint is the twin — never the instrument.

What a fingerprint is — and what it deliberately is not

Two different networks share no addresses, so nothing address-keyed can compare them. What can be compared is shape: role census, role-to-role edges, fan-out degree distribution, and, from iteration 15, the share of edges that leave the network. Each axis is reported separately, with key-set overlap (Jaccard) and distribution shape (total variation distance) kept apart.

Two kinds of number, running in opposite directions

Each axis reports both, and mixing them up inverts the reading — so it is worth stating plainly:

MeasureKindPerfectAnswers
Jaccardsimilarity1.000 Does the twin have the same vocabulary — the same roles, the same edges existing at all?
TVDdistance0.000 Are those things in the same proportions?
Neither is a match probability. Jaccard is set overlap; TVD is a distance between distributions.

Total variation distance is the standard statistical distance between two distributions — half the sum of absolute differences once both are normalised: 

TVD(P,Q) = ½ Σ |P(x) − Q(x)|        range 0 … 1

Two ways to read it. As transport: the share of probability mass you would have to move to turn one distribution into the other — which is why the number converts directly into “how many hosts are in the wrong role”. As a bound: the largest gap in probability the two distributions can assign to any single event, so at 0.016 no question of the form “what role is a random host?” gets answers more than 1.6 points apart. It is also scale-free, which is what lets a 122-host network be compared against a 121-host one without either dominating.

In plain terms: you would have to reassign about 1.6% of the twin's hosts — call it two of 121 — to make its role mix identical to the source's. Half of that residual is the two roles the twin deliberately omits (no capture supplies an AD forest name; nothing serves Proxmox 8006 inside a VM), and most of the rest is having 103 endpoints where the source has 102. That axis was finished at iteration 14; what remained was exclusions, not error.

The five dimensions, defined

Every number on the scoreboard comes from one script, shape_fingerprint.py, and shares one apparatus. Both captures are reduced to a materialised IR: entities — hosts, each stamped by the sensor as local or not, placed on a segment, and given an inferred role — and edge rows, one per source, destination and protocol per 60-second window, so a ten-minute span is about eleven windows. One predicate gates every locality-sensitive axis: an entity is local if the sensor said so and its segment is not in the external override, which on the twin side is the sink's CIDR only. An endpoint the materialiser could not resolve counts as external, deliberately.

1 Role census hosts to re-role

The distribution of local, placed, roled hosts over roles. TVD reads directly as the share of hosts you would have to re-role to match; Jaccard shows roles the twin lacks or invents. It says nothing about how hosts talk. Effectively finished at i14 (0.016), with the residual being roles the twin deliberately omits; i19's 0.057 is three real resolvers losing their role evidence.

2 Role edges conversation mix, all destinations

Every edge row whose two endpoints both carry a role becomes a key src_role→dst_role; the axis is the distribution over those keys, external destinations included. Two blind spots drove the loop: it rewards manufacturing local traffic to match an externally dominated total (i14's retraction), and it moves with role attribution rather than with traffic, since a host's inferred role relabels every edge landing on it (i17–i19). Its fall from 0.878 to 0.406 at i19 was 0.411 from a catalog line and 0.005 from the fresh capture.

3 Role edges, local the deciding axis

The same count restricted to rows whose destination is local under the gate; the twin's sink edges are excluded. This is the subset an air-gapped twin can actually produce, and the axis that decides convergence. It is the one that read as a band, and section 04 gives the reason: 12 of the source's 18 local keys cannot exist in the twin.

4 Fan-out, local how widely hosts talk

Per source host, the number of edge rows to local destinations, bucketed and then distributed: 

bucket   1   |   2–5   |   6–20   |   21+        (edge rows out of the host)

It compares how widely hosts talk, not to whom. Degree counts rows, so one peer over three protocols counts three. The local variant exists because the source's hosts are locally sparse — most of their peers are external. Its i16 reading of 0.162 was inflated by ICMP rows that were really dead-port replies; the clean value is 0.290.

5 External edge share outbound share vs the source

The fraction of all edge rows whose destination is not local — counting unresolved endpoints and, on the twin, the sink segment. A proportion rather than a distance, judged by closeness to the source's 0.848 from either side. It is role-free, so a catalog change cannot move it, and it is the axis the sink was built to move.

Two things the five have in common are worth stating. Every one of them counts edge rows, so none sees protocol, bytes, timing or session shape: the twin's byte-pusher DNS and a real query score identically. And they are all shape, never content. That is why the fidelity specification in section 05 keeps all of them as one behavioural row with a tolerance, rather than as the objective.

Why there is no single score

One blended number would have hidden every important result in this run. In iteration 10 a change improved the target axis and regressed three others tenfold. In iteration 13 an improvement lowered edge-set overlap because the twin stopped emitting fake edges it had been sharing with the source. In iteration 19 the target axis halved while the deciding axis regressed. A scalar would have moved slightly in the “right” direction every time and told nobody anything.

02The trajectory

Iterations 0–9 did the coarse work: role coverage went from 7.8% to 86.5%, and a cast that had been re-creating the lab's own gateway address on a LAN leg — killing egress — was fixed. Iterations 10–14 are where the fidelity question got sharp. Iteration 15 gave the twin an internet edge and a new 138-VM identity; 16 through 19 ran as traffic-tier changes against that one standing twin, its config byte-identical each time, so the iterations are comparable at all.

03The ledger

i10 Activity banding net regression

The source's generic-endpoint role spans three orders of magnitude — median 2,012 bytes per window, max 2,862,768, a 1,423× spread. One persona synthesised from the role mean made every host identical, so the twin had no heavy hitters. Splitting each role into heavy/base/light bands, each synthesised from its own band's observations, produced genuinely differentiated rates.

It improved the target axis and regressed three others — fan-out tenfold. Right diagnosis, wrong lever: banding fixes how much each host talks; the dominant error was how many peers it talks to.

i11 Measured emit budgets 5 of 6 axes

The synthesiser gave every persona up to twelve emits. Measured against the source's own edge-rows-per-host, that single constant was wrong in both directions at once — over-serving the twin's most numerous role and starving its busiest: 

role                source   twin
generic-endpoint       3.0    8.0   2.7x too chatty
corp-workstation      16.0    5.0   3x too quiet
dns-resolver           6.0    5.5   already right

Per-role budgets replaced the constant. The 6–20 fan-out bucket fell from 104 hosts to 7.

i12 Weight-ranked selection flat

A budget says how many emits; selection decides which. Ranking by dial-port frequency was blind to how much conversation a port carried. Ranking by observed flows fixed each persona in isolation — and the aggregate barely moved, because a role edge is Σ(hosts × emits) and band population dominates that sum.

It also became clear the channel was broken: with nothing listening, every edge was a two-packet refused connect. Tuning proportions measured through failures would have been fitting noise, and any fit achieved against refusals would have to be undone once conversations completed. That deferral was the most consequential judgement of the run.

i13 Listeners 6 of 7 axes

Nothing in the twin answered. Every client emit at a service role hit a bare VM whose kernel returned ICMP port-unreachable, so the twin's most common protocol was an error message: 

before : icmp 225, udp 148, tcp 145, ntp 39, ssl 3, dns 1
after  : udp 149, icmp 141, tcp  99, http 35, dns 2, ssl 2
source : tcp 446, ssl 422, ntp 176, dns 87, udp 85, icmp 76

A synthesised responder gave each dialled role a listener on exactly the ports something dials at it. http appeared for the first time; 103 fabricated reverse edges vanished. The residual matched a known bug exactly — the host whose listener worked improved, the host whose listener silently failed did not move.

i14 Proportional budgets — and the correction misleading win

Allocating each budget across destination roles in the source's proportions closed the dominant error almost exactly as projected: generic-endpoint→web-tier went 134 → 228 against a source 298. Best scores of the run so far. Zero twin-only roles.

Then splitting the source's edges by destination locality showed what that target really was: 

source role edge                   local  external
generic-endpoint->web-tier             3       295
corp-workstation->web-tier             0        76
generic-endpoint->dns-resolver        38        31
generic-endpoint->domain-controller   29         0

202 of the source's 204 web-tier entities are external. The target being chased was 295 internet edges and 3 local ones, and the fit drove the twin to manufacture 225 local web-tier edges against a source that has 3. The aggregate converged while the structure diverged. The local variant of the axis was added here and became the one that decides.

i15 The internet sink outbound axis moves

The twin got somewhere external to spend its outbound share: four sink roles held by 1, 2, 4 and 8 hosts — 15 VMs on a dedicated segment — so a persona composes its external fan-out from a binary subset. It is reached by role, never by address; the persona schema has no address field, so nothing in the twin can name a destination outside it. That is the safety constraint from the earlier recommendation, made structural rather than chosen. The old twin was destroyed and a 138-VM successor stood up.

The external edge share went 0.110 → 0.805 against a source of 0.848, and all-edges fan-out halved. The local axes did not move: role_edges_local 0.595 → 0.618. The sink fixed where traffic goes, not what the twin's hosts say to each other.

i16 Population-level allocation unreadable

Budgets were re-derived at the population level, with -local and -chatter persona variants so a role's local presence could be carried by a few hosts rather than smeared across all of them. Local fan-out appeared to collapse to 0.162, the best number of the loop, while the deciding axis went the other way to 0.742, the worst.

Both were wrong, in the same direction, for the same reason — but that could not be read from the numbers. The projection said local fan-out should land near 0.290; it was dismissed as overfitting. It was not.

i17 Emit ports and responder ports must move together the repair

The allocator had changed which ports carried the sink emits, and the responders were deliberately held back so the repo would mirror deployed reality. 228 emits landed on dead ports. Each cost twice: the conversation it should have produced was missing, and the guest's kernel answered with an ICMP port-unreachable that Zeek recorded with the replier as originator — scored as a local edge, the exact axis being judged. 

ICMP local rows                     220
web-tier->generic-endpoint          197   predicted 197
dns-resolver->generic-endpoint       23   predicted  23

Predicted from row counting before the run, measured exactly. The correct responders were pushed to 19 guests and verified in-guest with ss -lnH -tu, never trusted from a unit's is-active. Re-measured with the personas untouched: external share 0.886, local fan-out 0.290 — precisely the projection iteration 16 had rejected. A projection that disagrees with a corrupted measurement has not been refuted.

i18 Protocol fidelity drives role attribution best of the loop

Of the 24 protocol names the emit ranker could produce, 19 were tokens the observed edges never carry — https, kerberos, ldap, smb — so those ports scored a permanent zero and always sorted last. Port 443 could not spend the 243 rows of TLS weight it had earned; port 53 could, and won. The twin dialled the sink on 53 773 times against a source whose DNS is 17 of 711 outbound rows.

An alias table gave every emittable protocol an explicit decision. Outbound rows at 443 went 0 → 168, and role_edges_local reached 0.533, the best of the loop. But the sink still read as a DNS resolver, because the role catalog is first-match-wins and volume never enters it — which relabels hundreds of edges at once. That was the correction to a deferred gap: a protocol mix does not change how many edges exist, but it changes which ports carry them, which decides a host's inferred role, which relabels every edge landing on it.

i19 Rows, not flows — and the catalog's tie target hit, a trade

Two halves. The emit ranking sorted ports by observed flows while every fingerprint axis counts edge rows; DNS is many small flows per row, TLS fewer and fatter, so the ranking was sorted on a quantity the score does not use — 53 won on flows in all six persona groups, 443 won on rows in five. And the catalog decided the sink's role by declaration order on a tie between a 53/udp row and a 443/tcp row.

The obvious fix — require a second port for a DNS resolver — was built, then measured against the source, then not taken: 13 of the source's 15 resolvers carry {53/udp} as their entire listen evidence, so the rule would have stripped the reference's own resolvers from 15 to 1. Declaring the 443 row first flips exactly the 15 sink hosts and zero source hosts, brute-forced over every listen subset. Re-scoring the iteration-18 capture under that one line gave role_edges 0.411; the fresh capture measured 0.406.

Target hit: 14 of 15 sinks read as web-tier. And two of the three must-not-regress axes regressed — role_edges_local 0.533 → 0.593, external share 0.864 → 0.815 away from the source's 0.848. By the iteration's own rule, a trade. The loop closed here.

04Why the deciding axis reads as a band

Three ceilings were named at iteration 14 as things tuning could not reach. Iteration 15 lowered one. The other two stood, and iteration 19 showed what they add up to.

1 · No internet edge — closed by the sink

This was the dominant ceiling, and it explains why fan-out was the one axis that never improved through iteration 14 while everything around it did. The diagram below is the twin as it was then; the sink is what changed it.

2 · Persona emits are port-shaped, not protocol-faithful

The scripted driver pushes arbitrary bytes at a port number. So the twin's BIND9 receives garbage on :53, never answers a real query, and Zeek records a bare udp exchange. The source is ssl/dns/ntp-shaped; the twin cannot be, at any persona setting, until emits speak their protocols. Iteration 18 showed this is not only a realism problem: which ports carry the edges decides which role a host is inferred to hold, and that relabels every edge landing on it.

3 · Windows nodes get no responder

The synthesised responder is a POSIX shell action; delivered to a Windows guest it runs as PowerShell and dies on the first heredoc. Five nodes — including the domain controller — still refuse every connection and under-produce their forward edges.

What they add up to

At iteration 19, 12 of the source's 18 local role-edge kinds were absent from the twin entirely, and the twin overshot on the six it had — generic-endpoint→dns-resolver at 77 against a source 38. Every edge that would land on the domain controller cannot exist because the domain controller answers nothing; workstation-to-workstation traffic is not modelled at all. You cannot close a distribution gap by re-weighting mass when two thirds of the keys do not exist. Each cheap iteration redistributed the same six keys and traded one axis against another, which is exactly what the highlighted row in section 02 records. 

source local edge kinds            18
present in the twin                  6   (overshot)
absent from the twin                12   (DC answers nothing; no ws→ws)

05What golden means now

The loop closed on a correction to its objective, not on a score. The correction has three parts.

The fingerprint was never the goal, and it is not a proxy for it either. The platform exists to run attack-and-defense experiments. What makes those valid is that hosts speak real protocols so detection rules can fire, that the identity structure creates real attack paths, that segmentation constrains lateral movement the way the real network does, and that benign traffic is realistic enough to give a meaningful false-positive rate. A fan-out distance measures none of those. A twin could score zero on every axis and still be useless for detection engineering, because its DNS is a byte-pusher aimed at port 53.

Fidelity is a specification, not a distance. Eleven rows, each tagged with where its evidence can come from and how strong that evidence is:

The half the wire cannot see is an observation gap, not a reproduction gap. Configuration, state and security posture are nearly invisible to a sensor, and those are the rows an attack path depends on. The platform can already build a rich, deliberately weak Active Directory forest — promotion, users, service principals, dangerous ACL edges, delegation — live-verified down to a real DCSync in July. The cast never emits one, because the imprint has no directory evidence to derive it from. That is filled by injecting known information under the existing referenced evidence class: a directory export first, a host inventory second, device configs, security-tool baselines, operator-asserted facts. Posture is injected, never secrets.

Where the earlier recommendations went

01 An internet edge for the twin done · i15

Built as recommended, including the safety constraint made structural: the sink is reachable by role only, and moving its address range left every persona file byte-identical. External share went from 0.110 to within a few points of the source.

02 Protocol-faithful emitters client half builtpromoted to core

A drive: command persona driver now runs real clients — dig, curl, ntpdate, ping — with containment enforced on the final argument list, including the proxy and rc-file paths that a naive guard misses. Its server half, a sink that answers with real services, is the next piece, and it means a fresh twin rather than the standing one. This is the protocol row of the spec, and the one detection depends on.

03 A Windows responder superseded

An echo listener on Windows would make edges land; it would not make the domain controller a domain controller. Under the spec the fix is a real, promoted DC derived from a directory export, using machinery that already exists. The problem stands; the solution changed.

04 A fidelity scorecard, and a reference-ingest channel the new work

Per row: observed, referenced or imputed, against reproduced — computed from the cast's provenance sidecar, the deployed twin's published inventory and in-guest checks, and shown behind the imprint screen the console already has. On the observation side, a non-sensor ingest that takes point-in-time snapshots — a directory export, an inventory — into the identity and unseen tables that already exist, and a compiler step that emits the identity block the twin config already accepts. Drift becomes a re-imprint diffed against the golden spec, applied through the incremental update path.

05 Per-rule firewall policy promoted

The one reproduction gap injection cannot fill. The twin's routers ship a permissive forward policy and the conduit policy field turned out to be inert. Reachability is segments and routes today, not policy, so the security row cannot pass until the IR can express one.

Three words, three things

  • Fidelity — how faithfully a build reproduces the source at imprint time. An acceptance property, judged by the spec.
  • Golden — a build that passed the spec at a stated version. The artifact experiments run against; the scorecard lives in git beside it.
  • Drift — how far the live network has moved away from a golden twin since. A maintenance property: re-imprint later, diff against the spec.

The loop conflated all three by using a distance as a tuning objective. Distance survives as a measurement; it is no longer the objective. The spec's first golden is certified for attack-path and detection experiments, which is the capability the platform demonstrated in July and had not exercised since.

Two measurement traps worth knowing

The removal sweep runs after the executor, so an interrupted update leaves the entire removed generation live and still running personas. Capturing in that window blends two twin generations into one perfectly plausible fingerprint. And roles are baked at ingest time: a change to the role catalog is invisible to a fingerprint re-run and only shows up after re-ingesting the stored logs, which is how iteration 19's catalog line was attributed on the iteration-18 capture before the new one was taken.

07Method notes

  • Identical pipeline both sides. Same Zeek, same ingest window (60s), same materialisation. Only the harvest chunk size differs, and it does not affect the fingerprint.
  • Fixed 10-minute capture span across every iteration. Changing it would make the distances non-comparable and defeat the point of tracking them.
  • New axes are added, never substituted. fan_out_local, role_edges_local and the external share sit beside the originals so earlier iterations stay comparable.
  • One standing twin from iteration 15. Iterations 16 to 19 changed only personas and responders; the twin config was checked byte-identical each time rather than assumed, so a 138-VM rebuild never entered the comparison.
  • Failures must be loud. Silent-success bugs shaped this run repeatedly: an extraction error hidden behind a filter's exit code, a listener whose unit reported healthy while one port had crashed on bind, a fixed-point gate that skipped and passed. A responder serving some of its ports is worse than one that fails outright, because the fingerprint then blames the personas.
  • Predict before measuring. Iteration 17's artifact was sized to the row before the run; iteration 19's catalog line was scored on the previous capture before the new one. When a projection and a measurement disagree, the question is which of the two is wrong — and the default guess went the wrong way once already in this loop.

The standing twin offers 1.33 MB/s against the source's own 1.330 and remains deployed as the first subject of the scorecard. Every iteration's fingerprint store and the loop's inputs are kept outside the scratch directories that produced them.
