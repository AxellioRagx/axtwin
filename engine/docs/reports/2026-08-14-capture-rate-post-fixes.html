The small trace at l2–l4 is 2 packets in each case, on the host's gretap transmit queue. It is not rate-correlated — 2 packets at 22 Mbit/s and 2 at 1.5 Gbit/s, while the two heaviest lossless rungs show none at all — which argues against congestion and points at a per-frame property, most likely occasional oversized frames from the twin's background AD traffic exceeding the tunnel MTU. l2's percentage looks worse only because its denominator is small (2 of 240k).

What moved the ceiling

Sink NIC multiqueue

The capture NIC was single-queue, so a 32-core appliance ran every mirrored frame through one virtio receive ring. queues=8 hot-applied with no reboot and no loss of the static transport address. The sink has not dropped a packet since, at any rate this twin can produce — every vhost, nic rx and decap rx counter in the table below is zero.

Gretap qdisc, now adapter-managed

A new gretap inherited the node default, fq_codel — an AQM that deliberately drops to hold latency down, which is precisely wrong for a capture path. attach-gre-tunnel now asserts a deep FIFO itself, in its idempotent section, so adopted gretaps heal too and no manual re-assert is needed after a tap deploy.

What limits it now: all 17 tapped ports mirror into one single-queue gretap netdev. At rung l7 the tc mirred action logged 123,260 overlimits — mirrored copies the tunnel could not accept — while the sink stayed perfectly clean. Raising the ceiling further means per-sensor gretap fan-out or a multiqueue-capable tunnel device, not more sink capacity.

Two corrections to how this was measured

Per-host timestamps. A sample makes three sequential SSH round-trips. Stamping the whole sample once and dividing every stage by that single interval silently inflates rates when one read stalls: a stalled sink connection made the mirror stage report 588k pps against a gretap that had actually carried 138k. The harness now timestamps each host read separately, and the stages agree to within 0.2%.

Direction matters in a drop counter. Checking both directions at every stage is right in general and wrong at the decap device: a gre-cap tx_drop flagged "loss" on a 14 Mbit/s rung that had lost nothing. The loss column above is the directionally-correct subset.

Both were found while producing this report, which is the argument for re-measuring rather than reusing the earlier run's numbers: several of those were taken mid-change, with the qdisc applied by hand and the sink connection intermittent.
