The axquery twin-discovery loop

Can a network digital twin be discovered, deployed, and measured through axquery — the PacketXpress metadata engine — instead of Zeek? Iteration 1 ran the whole loop end to end on live hardware. Here is what it took, and what it found.

Loop converges — a fix improved fidelity

Bottom line. The loop runs to completion through axquery: discover → deploy → capture → re-analyze → re-discover → compare. The re-discovered twin reproduces the deployed twin's topology faithfully — host count, segments, and the major roles all land. Its own synthetic traffic, though, under-exercises its declared services: a listen port is invisible to a traffic-based re-discovery unless a client actually connects to it. That gap is the concrete thing the loop exists to find.

Why the services fall short

A declared listen port only appears in a traffic-based re-discovery when a client actually connects to it. The role personas emit only a subset of client traffic, so RDP, Kerberos, HTTPS, NTP and RPC listeners are never exercised on the wire — and what is never dialed is never seen.

A confounder amplifies it: the re-capture was a 12-minute, low-rate window against the source's two hours, so sparse services drop out first.

04 · What it took

Findings from the first run

  • axquery's rich data had to be wired in aximprint · committed

    The stock axquery ingest emitted only flows and sensor-health — 2 of 6 event types. The extension added a UDP-listener fix and identity from /api/events, so the discovered twin gained real UDP services (DNS, NTP, DHCP) and an identity surface instead of an impoverished flow graph.

  • The analytics engine deadlocks on its own capture core fx-cap · worked around

    axanalytics unconditionally pins its main thread to CPU 29 — the live capture core — whose kernel migration path is wedged, so every start hangs and survives kill -9. Confining the cgroup with AllowedCPUs=0-23 makes the pin fail fast and the app roll back gracefully.

  • The metadb is a live capture, not a replay operational

    axanalytics only follows live; it will not read a historical window from the pxfs ring. The fresh metadb must be produced while traffic flows, so capture and traffic generation run concurrently.

  • Drift is the wrong comparator here by design

    aximprint's drift refuses a cross-vantage compare — a PIT is one tap point's view. Source-versus-twin fidelity is a structural question (roles, segments, services), not an entity diff, because the twin's identities are synthetic.

  • Persona traffic tapers after its burst observed

    The mirror rate fell from ~15k to ~1k packets/s once the personas' initial burst subsided — enough to starve sparse services during the capture window.

05 · Iteration 2 — the fix, applied

The loop converges

Iteration 1's fix was applied: a disco-exercise persona put 111 Linux client hosts onto the twin's own internal servers. On the next capture, service recovery jumped for every Linux-served service — the fidelity loop measurably converges.

ServiceportIter 1Iter 2intendedresult

edges 843 → 7,778 (~9×) — the internal-service connections that had been missing

Recovered — the Linux services

web-tier (nginx), dns-resolver (bind9) and file-server (Samba) accept the exercise connections, so HTTPS, HTTP, DNS and SMB are now re-discovered as servers.

The next-layer blocker — Windows Firewall

Kerberos, RPC and RDP stayed flat: they live on the Windows DC and workstations, whose firewall blocks inbound from the Linux clients. Iteration 3 opens those ports or runs the exercise from Windows — a precisely diagnosed residual, not a mystery.
